# Partnership Kickoff — Queensland (Plain-English Version)

> This version explains the same topics as the main kickoff doc in simple language. It keeps the structure and decisions but avoids jargon. Always check with a qualified lawyer, tax adviser, and lender before acting.

## Purpose

- Get everyone on the same page about goals, how we set up the investment vehicle, how we borrow money, how we make decisions, how we handle risk, and what happens next.
- Output: pick one or two ways to set up the entity (per deal vs ongoing), agree basic funding and loan settings, agree how we vote and report, and set a short pilot plan.

## What Is an SPV?

- An SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle) is a simple company or a trust used for one project or property. It helps separate risk so one project does not affect others. It also makes joining or leaving simpler.
- Choices:
  - One SPV per deal: clean and simple per project; a bit more admin.
  - An ongoing vehicle: keeps everything together over time; exits and valuations are trickier.
- Queensland checks: confirm property transfer duty, land tax rules, GST for building/selling or commercial rent, and AFAD (extra duty for some foreign buyers).

## Agenda (Outcomes)

- Kickoff: Agree why we’re doing this and how we measure success.
- Partner Goals: Each person’s goals, how long they want to invest, and risk comfort.
- Strategy: What types of properties, where, target returns, and how much debt we’re OK with.
- Structure Options: Shortlist 1–2 setup choices.
- Capital & Finance: How much each invests, loan settings, and cash reserves.
- Governance & Decisions: Who does what and how we vote.
- Risk & Due Diligence: What can go wrong and how we check deals.
- Next Steps: Owners, dates, expert reviews, and pilot timeline.

## Identifier Matrix (Quick Map)

- M-101 — Structure choice: trust with a company as trustee vs a company that owns the property; per-deal vs ongoing.
- M-201 — Finance: loan-to-value limits, debt coverage, interest-only vs principal-and-interest, guarantees, and linking loans across assets.
- M-301 — Governance: voting thresholds, roles, and information rights.
- M-401 — Risk: reserves and insurance settings.
- M-601 — Exit & liquidity: how long we hold, how we exit, buy/sell rules.
- M-701 — Ethics & conflicts: related-party rules and disclosure.

## Early Decisions To Lock

- Capital model (M-201): minimum commitments, reserves, and how extra cash calls work.
- Structure (M-101): per-deal SPVs vs one ongoing vehicle; unit trust + corporate trustee vs company.
- Governance (M-301): voting levels for small/medium/big decisions, key roles, reporting rights.
- Risk posture (M-401): max loan-to-value, stance on personal guarantees, if we link loans across properties, any rate hedging.
- Exit/liquidity (M-601): hold period, buy/sell mechanics, valuation method, any redemption options.
- Ethics/conflicts (M-701): how we handle deals with related parties and required disclosures.

## Structure Options (Pros/Cons)

- Simple Partnership (no company):
  - Pros: very easy and cheap; flexible.
  - Cons: unlimited personal liability; hard to add/exit partners; some lenders don’t like it.
  - Use when: tiny, high-trust group and very simple projects.
- Co-ownership (Tenants in Common / Joint Tenants):
  - Pros: direct title; simple.
  - Cons: financing can be harder; joint exposure; exits are clunky.
  - Use when: one simple asset with a small group.
- Unincorporated JV (contract):
  - Pros: flexible and project-limited; profits pass through.
  - Cons: bank comfort varies; some joint risk depending on terms.
  - Use when: bounded project scope, no new entity.
- Company (`Pty Ltd`):
  - Pros: limited liability; familiar to banks; clear governance.
  - Cons: company pays tax first; shareholder dividends later; duty on share transfers may apply.
  - Use when: multi-asset or ongoing program; banks prefer a company borrower.
- Unit Trust (with a company as trustee):
  - Pros: common for AU property; flexible distributions; unitholders may get CGT discounts (facts matter).
  - Cons: trust deeds are detailed; lenders have quirks; duty/landholder rules to check.
  - Use when: investor-friendly distributions and flexibility are desired.
- Syndication / MIS:
  - Pros: can include more investors and add professional management.
  - Cons: more rules for retail investors; licensing/AFSL may apply.
  - Use when: larger deals or third-party capital, often wholesale-only.
- SPV-per-deal vs ongoing:
  - Per-deal: cleaner exits and risk separation.
  - Ongoing: operational efficiency, but manage legacy assets and exits carefully.

## Finance & Lending (Plain)

- Serviceability: aim for a comfortable buffer so income covers debt (e.g., DSCR at or above about 1.25x after lender stress tests).
- LVR & guarantees: set a max loan-to-value per asset class; agree whether guarantees are personal or company-level and whether liability is shared or limited.
- Cross-collateralisation: prefer keeping loans separate; if we link them, cap exposure and set release rules.
- Interest profile: interest-only during renovation/stabilisation; switch to principal-and-interest later; consider rate protection if needed.
- Entities: lenders often ask for director guarantees with trusts/companies; start lender conversations early.

## Tax (Plain, get advice)

- Income vs capital: rent vs sale profits can be taxed differently; developments may be treated as regular business income.
- CGT: discounts depend on how long you hold and other facts; small business rules are special and conditional.
- GST: residential rent usually has no GST; new residential sales often do have GST (margin scheme may help); most commercial rent has GST if registered.
- QLD duty & land tax: duty on property transfers and sometimes on big changes in unit/share holdings; trust/company land tax rules differ; AFAD may apply to foreign buyers.
- Interest deductions: must link to the purpose of the loan; keep records clean.

## Risk & Due Diligence (Plain)

- Risks: market prices, interest rates, vacancies, renovation costs/delays, title/zoning, environment, refinancing, builder/tenant/lender risk, tax/legal changes, operations, deadlocks, fraud/cyber, cash shocks, major events, reputation, FX (if cross-border), key person, poor data.
- Mitigations: insurance, loan covenants, cash reserves, diversification, clear governance and approvals.

## Due Diligence & Data Room (Minimum)

- Property: title and searches, zoning/permits, environment checks, building & pest, utilities, strata/body corporate, rent roll and leases, operating costs, insurance claims history.
- Financial: last 12–24 months income/expenses, bank statements, tax returns, budgets, capex plan, stress tests on DSCR/LVR, comparable sales/rents, valuations.
- Legal/regulatory: contracts, easements/encroachments, code issues, licensing, AFAD checks.
- Business plan: scope, timeline, costs, contingencies, exit plans.
- Counterparties: builder/PM/vendor checks and references.
- Data room: summary, investment memo, model, photos/maps, survey, title, environmental reports, building & pest, leases/letters, rent roll, financials, insurance, permits, bids, draft loan terms.

## Operating Model (How we run it)

- Decision rights: small, medium, and big decisions with vote levels (e.g., 51% small; 75% medium; 90–100% big/reserved).
- Voting & quorum: vote by capital or by person; quorum example: more than two-thirds of capital.
- Information rights: monthly dashboard (income, debt coverage, occupancy), quarterly financials, annual plan; access to books.
- Cash management: who gets paid first (expenses → debt → reserves → preferred return if any → profit split);
  reserves for expenses, capex, interest, tax; rules for capital calls and what happens if someone doesn’t fund (dilution/penalty/loans).
- Conflicts & related parties: disclose, compare prices to market, get a vote from non-conflicted partners.
- On/Offboarding: KYC/AML, subscription documents, valuation method, buy/sell or redemption rules.

## Pilot Roadmap (First QLD Resi SPV)

- Stage 1: align objectives; shortlist structures; assign roles; set up shared workspace/data room; engage QLD legal/tax and lending broker.
- Stage 2: draft term sheet + decision matrix; pick a banking partner; set reserves and LVR/DSCR limits; prepare reporting templates.
- Stage 3: choose structure and QLD-specific settings; draft core agreements; form entity and open bank accounts (subject to advice).
- Stage 4: build pilot pipeline (3–5 deals); run due diligence; base model with sensitivities; get soft loan terms; committee picks pilot; schedule docs and capital calls.

## Glossary (Plain)

- LVR — Loan size divided by property value.
- DSCR — Income divided by debt payments; higher is safer.
- IO / P&I — Interest-only vs principal-and-interest loan payments.
- AFAD — Extra duty for certain foreign buyers in QLD.
- SPV — A simple entity for a single project or property.
- MIS — Managed Investment Scheme (a regulated fund for multiple investors).
