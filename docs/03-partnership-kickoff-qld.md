## Purpose

- Align prospective partners on goals, structure, finance, governance, risk, and next steps for property and other wealth-building deals in Queensland.
- Output: shortlist of structures (SPV-per-deal vs evergreen), draft decision matrix, initial capital/finance settings, and a pilot roadmap.

## What Is an SPV?

- Definition: A Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) is a single-purpose entity (commonly a `Pty Ltd` company or a unit trust with a corporate trustee) formed to hold one asset or project, ring-fencing liability and simplifying exits or partner changes.
- When to use:
  - SPV-per-deal: isolates risk per asset and provides clean buy/sell/finance options; adds admin per deal.
  - Evergreen vehicle: ongoing portfolio and operational continuity; introduces legacy/valuation and exit complexity.
- QLD context: Confirm transfer duty settings (incl. landholder duty on significant interests), land tax (trust/company rules), GST on developments and commercial leasing, and AFAD for foreign participants.

## Structure Options *(M-101)* — Pros/Cons (QLD/AU Lens)

- Simple Partnership (Unincorporated):
  - Pros: Simplest setup; minimal compliance (no ASIC, no separate tax return); direct control; flexible profit split via partnership agreement; low cost.
  - Cons: Unlimited personal liability for all partners; joint and several exposure; harder to bring in new partners or exit cleanly; lenders may be less comfortable; all partners taxed on their share of income regardless of distributions.
  - Use: Small, high-trust group; simple deals; minimal compliance appetite; willingness to accept personal liability trade-off.
- Tenants in Common / Joint Tenancy:
  - Pros: Simple, direct title; fractional shares (TIC).
  - Cons: Financing complexity, joint exposure, less flexible for scaling/exits.
  - Use: Small group, single asset, simple arrangements.
- Unincorporated JV (contract):
  - Pros: Flexible, pass-through economics, project-bounded.
  - Cons: Bank comfort varies; joint/several risk depending on terms.
  - Use: Defined project scope without creating a separate entity.
- `Pty Ltd` Company (shareholders):
  - Pros: Limited liability; lender familiarity; governance via constitution/shareholders’ agreement.
  - Cons: Potential double taxation (company + dividends); duty/landholder duty on share transfers at certain thresholds.
  - Use: Multi-asset or evergreen program; where bank/insurers prefer a corporate borrower.
- Unit Trust (with corporate trustee):
  - Pros: Common in AU property; distribution flexibility; potential CGT discount at unitholder level (subject to conditions).
  - Cons: Lender quirks; trust deed complexity; trust/landholder duty considerations in QLD; fixed vs discretionary nuances.
  - Use: AU deals with investor distribution preferences and flexibility needs.
- Syndication / Managed Investment Scheme (MIS):
  - Pros: Scales to more investors; professional governance.
  - Cons: ASIC MIS/AFSL obligations for retail; stricter compliance.
  - Use: Larger deals or third-party capital; often wholesale-only to avoid retail burdens.
- SPV-per-deal vs Evergreen:
  - SPV-per-deal: Ring-fence risk, clean exits, tailored partner mix.
  - Evergreen: Ongoing portfolio, operational efficiency; manage legacy/valuation and exits carefully.

## Finance & Lending *(M-201)* — AU Context

- Serviceability: Stress DSCR (e.g., ≥1.25x) with interest rate and vacancy buffers aligned to lender sensitisation.
- LVR/recourse: Set LVR caps per asset class; align on full/limited recourse and guarantees (personal vs corporate; proportional vs joint/several).
- Cross-collateralisation: Prefer ring-fencing; if used, cap exposure and set release tests.
- Interest profile: IO during value-add/stabilisation; transition to P&I post-stabilisation; consider rate hedging.
- Entities: Trusts/companies often require director guarantees and may receive tighter LVRs; build lender relationships early.

## Tax (High-Level — Obtain Advice)

- Income vs capital: Different treatments for rents vs gains; development may be on revenue account.
- CGT: Holding periods/discounts depend on facts; small business concessions are specific and conditional.
- GST: Residential rent generally input-taxed; new residential sales/development commonly taxable (margin scheme may apply); commercial leasing generally taxable if registered.
- QLD duties/land tax: Transfer duty (including potential landholder duty on significant interests); land tax settings differ for trusts/companies; AFAD for foreign acquirers.
- Interest deductibility: Purpose/nexus-based; maintain clean tracing.

## Risk Register *(M-401)* — Selected Risks & Mitigations

- Risks: Market/valuation; interest rate; vacancy/lease-up; construction cost/delay; title/zoning; environmental; liquidity/refinance; counterparty (builder/tenant/lender); regulatory/tax recharacterisation; operational (PM failures); governance deadlock; fraud/cyber; cash flow shocks; force majeure; ESG/reputation; FX (if cross-border); key person; data quality.
- Mitigations: Insurance (property/liability/D&O/construction/environmental/cyber), covenants (LVR/DSCR/distribution gates), reserves (opex/capex/interest/tax), diversification, governance (delegations, dual approvals, independent reviews).

## Due Diligence & Data Room (Minimum)

- Property: Title/search/survey; zoning/permits; environmental (Phase I/II); building & pest; utilities; strata/body corporate; rent roll/lease audits; O&M costs; insurance claims history.
- Financial: T-12/T-24 P&L; bank statements; tax returns; budgets; capex plan; DSCR/LVR stress; comps; valuation/appraisal.
- Legal/regulatory: Contracts; easements/encroachments; code issues; licensing; foreign acquirer issues (AFAD).
- Business plan: Scope, timeline, costings, contingencies, exit assumptions.
- Counterparties: GC/PM/vendor diligence; references; financial standing.
- Data room: Exec summary; investment memo; model; photos/maps; survey; title; ESA; B&P; leases/estoppels; rent roll; T-12/T-24; insurance; permits; bids; draft lender terms.

## Operating Model *(M-301)*

- Decision rights: Ordinary vs major vs reserved; examples: 51% ordinary; 75% major; 90–100% reserved.
- Voting/quorum: By capital or per person; quorum e.g., >66% capital; written resolutions.
- Information rights: Monthly dashboard (NOI/DSCR/occupancy), quarterly financials, annual plan; books access.
- Cash management: Waterfall (opex → debt → reserves → pref → splits), reserves policy; capital call mechanics and dilution/penalty rules.
- Conflicts/related parties: Disclosure; benchmarking; disinterested approval (e.g., 75%).
- On/Offboarding: KYC/AML, subscription docs; valuation method; buy-sell/redemptions.

## Glossary (Key Acronyms)

- **AFAD** — Additional Foreign Acquirer Duty (QLD transfer duty surcharge for certain foreign buyers).
- **APRA** — Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (banking and lending standards regulator).
- **ASIC** — Australian Securities and Investments Commission (corporate, markets and MIS/AFSL regulator).
- **ATO** — Australian Taxation Office.
- **CGT** — Capital Gains Tax.
- **DD** — Due Diligence.
- **DSCR** — Debt Service Cover Ratio (net operating income divided by debt service).
- **GST** — Goods and Services Tax.
- **IC** — Investment Committee.
- **IO / P&I** — Interest-Only vs Principal and Interest loan repayment profiles.
- **JV** — Joint Venture.
- **LMI** — Lenders Mortgage Insurance (usually when LVR is high, e.g., >80% on resi loans).
- **LVR** — Loan to Value Ratio (loan amount ÷ asset value).
- **MIS** — Managed Investment Scheme.
- **NOI** — Net Operating Income (rental income minus operating expenses, before interest/tax).
- **PM** — Property Manager.
- **QRO** — Queensland Revenue Office.
- **SPV / SPE** — Special Purpose Vehicle / Entity (single-purpose entity to hold a specific asset or project).
