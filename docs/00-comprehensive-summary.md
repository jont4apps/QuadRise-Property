## 1. Investment Philosophy & Strategies

### Core Investment Philosophy

The partnership follows a **balanced portfolio approach** combining:
- **High-yield "cash cow" properties:** Support serviceability and cash flow
- **High-capital-growth assets:** Build long-term equity
- **Manufactured equity:** Focus on value-add potential (cosmetic renovations, structural extensions, subdivision) to force appreciation independent of market cycles
- **Strategic market entry:** Target suburbs with leading indicators of growth (upcoming infrastructure, gentrification, tight rental vacancy rates <2%)

### Acquisition & Exit Strategies

#### 1. Buy and Hold (Long-Term Wealth)
- Acquire assets in high-demand locations
- Leverage negative gearing initially, transitioning to positive cash flow over time
- **Structure Consideration:** Discretionary Trust (for asset protection and flexibility)

#### 2. Develop and Sell (Trading/Flipping)
- Acquire sites for townhouse/duplex development or subdivision
- Focus on speed of execution to minimize holding costs
- **Structure Consideration:** Company structure (capped tax rate ~25-30% on trading profits)

#### 3. BRRRR Strategy (Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat)
- Buy distressed or undervalued assets
- Renovate to increase valuation and rental yield
- Refinance to pull out equity for the next deposit

#### 4. Subdivide and Split (Land & Build Strategy)
- Identify properties with subdivision potential (corner blocks, wide frontages)
- **Execution:** Subdivide into two lots
  - **Lot 1 (Vacant Land):** Sell immediately to generate cash flow and reduce principal debt
  - **Lot 2 (House + Land):** Retain existing dwelling or build new to sell as finished product
- **Goal:** Reduce holding costs and risk by securing early capital return from land sale

#### 5. Multi-Unit Development (Townhouse/Unit Strategy)
- Acquire larger blocks or amalgamated sites zoned for medium density (R3/R4)
- **Execution:** Design and construct a complex of townhouses, villas, or units
- **Goal:** Maximize site yield and profit margins through economies of scale in construction

### Financial & Tax Optimization Goals

- **Tax Efficiency:** Utilize corporate structures for active trading (development) to manage CGT and income tax
- **Serviceability Management:** Maintain healthy LVR and DSCR to allow for continuous lending
- **Partner Leverage:** Pool capital and borrowing capacity to access higher-grade commercial or multi-residential assets

## 2. Ownership Structures & Legal Considerations

### Available Structures

#### Company (`Pty Ltd`)
- **Tax Rate:** 25-30% (company tax rate)
- **Distribution:** Profits distributed as dividends (franking credits may offset double taxation)
- **Pros:** Limited liability, lender familiarity, clear governance via constitution/shareholders' agreement
- **Cons:** Potential double taxation, director duties, ongoing ASIC/ATO compliance, duty/landholder duty on share transfers
- **Use Cases:** Multi-asset or evergreen program, where banks/insurers prefer corporate borrower

#### Trust (Discretionary/Unit Trust)
- **Tax Rate:** Income distributed to beneficiaries, taxed at their marginal rates
- **Distribution:** Flexible distributions per trust deed
- **Pros:** Asset protection, tax planning flexibility, potential CGT discount at beneficiary level, common in AU property
- **Cons:** Complex setup/administration, compliance with trust deeds and ATO rules, lender quirks, trust/landholder duty considerations
- **Use Cases:** Investor-friendly distributions, pass-through economics, flexible capital accounts, property syndications

#### Partnership (Unincorporated)
- **Tax Rate:** Income split among partners, taxed at individual rates
- **Distribution:** Profit share per partnership agreement
- **Pros:** Simplest setup, minimal compliance (no ASIC, no separate tax return), direct control, flexible profit split, low cost
- **Cons:** Unlimited personal liability, joint and several exposure, harder to bring in new partners or exit cleanly, lenders may be less comfortable
- **Use Cases:** Small, high-trust group; simple deals; minimal compliance appetite; willingness to accept personal liability trade-off

#### Joint Venture (Unincorporated)
- **Tax Rate:** Taxed according to each party's structure (company, trust, individual)
- **Distribution:** As per agreement
- **Pros:** Flexible, pass-through economics, project-bounded
- **Cons:** Bank comfort varies, joint/several risk depending on terms, complex agreements
- **Use Cases:** Defined project scope without creating a separate entity

#### Tenants in Common / Joint Tenancy
- **Pros:** Simple, direct title, fractional shares (TIC)
- **Cons:** Financing complexity, joint exposure, less flexible for scaling/exits
- **Use Cases:** Small group, single asset, simple arrangements

#### Syndication / Managed Investment Scheme (MIS)
- **Pros:** Scales to more investors, professional governance
- **Cons:** ASIC MIS/AFSL obligations for retail, stricter compliance
- **Use Cases:** Larger deals or third-party capital, often wholesale-only to avoid retail burdens

### Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) Strategy

**Definition:** An SPV is a single-purpose entity (commonly a `Pty Ltd` company or unit trust with corporate trustee) formed to hold one asset or project, ring-fencing liability and simplifying exits or partner changes.

**Options:**
- **SPV-per-deal:** Isolates risk per asset, provides clean buy/sell/finance options, adds admin per deal
- **Evergreen vehicle:** Ongoing portfolio and operational continuity, introduces legacy/valuation and exit complexity

### Partnership vs Joint Venture Distinction

**Key Differences:**
- **Partnership:** Not a separate legal entity but requires tax return; partners include share of net income in individual returns; continuing business relationship
- **Joint Venture:** Does not lodge tax return; each joint venturer lodges separate return; participants share product/output rather than profits; created for specific economic project; participants usually liable for their own debts

**For GST:** Joint venturers may form a single group; transactions between group members ignored for GST purposes; representative member manages GST affairs.

### Financing Options for Property Partnerships

#### 1. Single Joint Loan
- **Liability:** All partners jointly and severally liable for entire debt
- **Borrowing Power:** Combined income and assets assessed
- **Impact:** Can reduce individual borrowing capacity for future investments

#### 2. Individual "Property Share" Loans
- **Liability:** Each partner responsible only for their portion
- **Borrowing Power:** Each partner assessed individually
- **Impact:** Only share of loan factored into personal borrowing capacity
- **Ownership:** Common for tenants in common with unequal ownership shares

**Critical Requirement:** Formal partnership agreement outlining financial contributions, ownership shares, management responsibilities, sale procedures, exit mechanisms, and dispute resolution.

## 3. Tax & Legal/Compliance Considerations

### Tax Treatment by Structure

| Structure | Tax Rate | Distribution Method | Double-Taxation Risk | Legal/Compliance Risks |
|-----------|----------|---------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Company | 25-30% | Dividends | Possible, offset by franking credits | ASIC/ATO compliance, director duties |
| Trust | Beneficiary rate | Distributions | No | Trustee duties, trust law compliance |
| Partnership | Individual rate | Profit share | No | Unlimited liability, partnership agreement |
| Joint Venture | Varies | As per agreement | Varies | JV contract, dispute risk |

### Partner Tax Obligations (Partnership)

A partnership:
- Has its own TFN
- Must lodge annual partnership return showing all business income and deductions
- Must apply for ABN and use it for all business activities
- Must register for GST if annual GST turnover ≥ $75,000 ($150,000 for not-for-profit)
- May be required to lodge business activity statements
- **Does not pay tax** — each partner reports their share of net partnership income/loss in their own tax return

### Key Tax Considerations

#### Income vs Capital
- Different treatments for rents vs gains
- Development may be on revenue account

#### Capital Gains Tax (CGT)
- Holding periods/discounts depend on facts
- Small business concessions are specific and conditional
- CGT discount may apply at unitholder level for trusts (subject to conditions)

#### GST
- **Residential rent:** Generally input-taxed
- **New residential sales/development:** Commonly taxable (margin scheme may apply)
- **Commercial leasing:** Generally taxable if registered

#### Queensland Duties & Land Tax
- **Transfer duty:** Payable on property acquisitions
- **Landholder duty:** May trigger on significant interest changes in landholding entities (unit/share transfers)
- **Land tax:** Settings differ for trusts/companies; rates/thresholds and surcharges may vary
- **AFAD:** Additional Foreign Acquirer Duty for foreign acquirers

#### Interest Deductibility
- Purpose/nexus-based; maintain clean tracing

## 4. Partnership & Syndicate Options

### Partnership Types

#### Joint Ventures (JV)
- **Equity JV:** One partner provides capital, another provides expertise
- **Landowner JV:** Landowner provides property, developer manages construction/sales
- **Development Partner JV:** Multiple developers pool resources for larger projects

#### Property Syndicates
- Group of investors pool funds to purchase property
- Managed by professional syndicator
- Most operate under managed investment rules for wholesale investors in Australia

#### Co-ownership with Family/Friends
- Pooling money with trusted individuals for greater purchasing power
- Common structures: tenants in common, joint tenants

### Pros and Cons of Property Partnerships

| Aspect | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Financial** | Pooled funds increase buying power; shared responsibility lowers cost | All partners share risks; joint liability for debts |
| **Expertise** | Diverse skills; reduced workload/stress | Disputes over management, workload imbalance |
| **Flexibility** | Access to more opportunities; diversification | Conflicting goals, difficult exits |
| **Legal** | Clear agreements define roles, profit-sharing, exit strategies | Complex tax, stamp duty, and legal implications; survivorship issues |
| **Personal** | Emotional support, shared goals | Relationship breakdowns, financial stress |

### Protection Strategies

1. **Choose partners carefully:** Trust, aligned goals, risk tolerance
2. **Establish formal legal agreements:** Roles, contributions, profit/loss, dispute resolution
   - **Note:** If no written agreement, income and losses are equally distributed between partners
3. **Create explicit exit strategies**
4. **Seek expert advice:** Property lawyer, financial adviser, mortgage broker
5. **Consider ownership structure:** Tenants in common vs. joint tenants
6. **Explore split loans** for financial independence

## 6. Finance & Lending (Australian Context)

### Serviceability

- **Stress DSCR:** Target ≥1.25x with interest rate and vacancy buffers aligned to lender sensitisation
- **LVR/Recourse:** Set LVR caps per asset class; align on full/limited recourse and guarantees (personal vs corporate; proportional vs joint/several)
- **Cross-collateralisation:** Prefer ring-fencing; if used, cap exposure and set release tests

### Interest Profile

- **IO (Interest-Only):** During value-add/stabilisation phase
- **P&I (Principal & Interest):** Transition post-stabilisation
- **Rate Hedging:** Consider if needed

### Entity Considerations

- Trusts/companies often require director guarantees
- May receive tighter LVRs
- Build lender relationships early

### Capital Model

- **Minimum commitments:** Define upfront
- **Reserves policy:** Target months of opex/interest; capex program; tax set-asides
- **Capital call mechanics:** Notice period; cure window; consequences (dilution/penalty/loans)

## 7. Governance & Decision-Making

### Decision Classes & Voting Thresholds

#### Ordinary Decisions
- **Examples:** Operating budget within limits; vendor selection; contracts under delegated authority; minor capex; property management decisions
- **Proposed Threshold:** 51% of capital (or majority of partners)
- **Quorum:** >66% of capital present
- **Delegations:** Manager/Treasurer up to specified limits

#### Major Decisions
- **Examples:** Acquisitions/disposals; new debt/refinance; distributions outside policy; capex above threshold; hedging
- **Proposed Threshold:** 75% of capital
- **Quorum:** >75% of capital present
- **Additional:** Written resolutions permitted

#### Reserved Matters
- **Examples:** Admit/exit partners; amend constitutive documents; change strategy/mandate; related-party transactions beyond caps; dissolution
- **Proposed Threshold:** 90-100% of capital
- **Quorum:** >80-90% of capital present
- **Conflict Management:** Disinterested approvals for related-party items

### Information & Reporting Rights

- **Monthly ops dashboard:** NOI, DSCR, occupancy, arrears, covenant headroom

## 9. Structure Comparison: Unit Trust vs Pty Ltd

### Unit Trust with Corporate Trustee

**Pros:**
- Common in AU property
- Distribution flexibility
- Potential CGT discount at unitholder level (subject to conditions)
- Pass-through economics
- Flexible capital accounts

**Cons:**
- Lender quirks
- Trust deed complexity
- Trust/landholder duty considerations in QLD
- Fixed vs discretionary nuances

**Use Cases:**
- Investor-friendly distributions
- Pass-through economics
- Property syndications (often wholesale)

### Proprietary Limited Company (`Pty Ltd`)

**Pros:**
- Limited liability
- Lender familiarity
- Clear governance via constitution/shareholders' agreement
- Straightforward financial reporting

**Cons:**
- Potential double taxation (company + dividends)
- Duty/landholder duty on share transfers at certain thresholds
- No individual-level CGT discount inside company

**Use Cases:**
- Operating company or evergreen vehicle
- Straightforward governance
- Institutional lender comfort

### Key Decision Factors (QLD)

- **Duty/Landholder:** Consider implications of unit/share transfers vs direct property transfers
- **Land Tax:** Trust vs company rate/surcharge differences
- **GST:** Development and commercial leasing registration; margin scheme eligibility
- **Lender Terms:** Borrowing powers in deed/constitution; guarantees; LVR/DSCR covenants
- **Exit Path:** Unit/share sale vs asset sale; tax/duty consequences

## 13. Key Acronyms & Glossary

- **AFAD** — Additional Foreign Acquirer Duty (QLD transfer duty surcharge for certain foreign buyers)
- **APRA** — Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (banking and lending standards regulator)
- **ASIC** — Australian Securities and Investments Commission (corporate, markets and MIS/AFSL regulator)
- **ATO** — Australian Taxation Office
- **CGT** — Capital Gains Tax
- **DD** — Due Diligence
- **DSCR** — Debt Service Cover Ratio (net operating income divided by debt service)
- **GST** — Goods and Services Tax
- **IC** — Investment Committee
- **IO / P&I** — Interest-Only vs Principal and Interest loan repayment profiles
- **JV** — Joint Venture
- **LMI** — Lenders Mortgage Insurance (usually when LVR is high, e.g., >80% on resi loans)
- **LVR** — Loan to Value Ratio (loan amount ÷ asset value)
- **MIS** — Managed Investment Scheme
- **NOI** — Net Operating Income (rental income minus operating expenses, before interest/tax)
- **PM** — Property Manager
- **QRO** — Queensland Revenue Office
- **SPV / SPE** — Special Purpose Vehicle / Entity (single-purpose entity to hold a specific asset or project)
